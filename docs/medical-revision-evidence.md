# Medical Foundations for Reviewing ASTRA

Checked on November 8, 2026. This document fixes the boundaries of **simulated training** and sources for a specific case of external bleeding near the elbow. It is not a NASA protocol or a treatment authorization and does not grant access to the task.

## Origin of the Case

In the fictional profile, there is a handwritten note about a cut near the elbow and dark blood; it is not marked as synthetic. The exact text, time, identifier, and source remain in the local database and are presented in the complaints section. The fictional nature of the **profile** and the origin of the **note** are different indicators. There is no examination, volume, or rate of bleeding, depth of the wound, information about a lodged object, movement, and sensitivity of the hand or laboratory analysis. These unknowns cannot be renamed as measurement results or a diagnosis.

Since speed and volume are not specified, it is not possible to classify the bleeding as either safe or life-threatening. It is not permissible to apply a tourniquet solely based on this phrase: [Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/bleeding-life-threatening-external) limits its use to situations of life-threatening bleeding from a limb and the application preparation. It is not permissible to write about calling ground emergency medical services as an available action on the ISS; use onboard roles and mission standard procedures in the interface.

## Mission Evaluation Boundary

[NASA-STD-3001, 5.0 Training](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-0-training/) requires basic crew training in first aid and specialized training for the Crew Medical Officer, but does not confirm the appointment of such an officer in the fictional profile ASTRA. [NASA-STD-3001, 6.0 Medical Operations](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-medical-operations/) requires programmatically defined medical requirements; for EVA, it describes the approval of ground medical support prior to each extravehicular activity. Therefore, ASTRA can only show an assessment **based on explicitly configured training criteria**. Without them, the result would be "requires verification by responsible personnel," not a real medical clearance. None of the found public NASA documents confirm a specific first aid protocol for a cut on the forearm in space.

## Audit of the Existing Catalog

`backend/reference_docs/ASTRA_DEMO_GUIDANCE.json` version `1.2` contains actions for several training candidates, including head trauma, and URL sources for individual statements. There is no separate card for a cut on the forearm. This is a catalog of **general training recommendations**, not an onboard protocol. Its phrase about "emergency care" in the head trauma card requires adaptation to the mission; a simple transfer of NHS/Red Cross text about ground emergency services to the ISS would be inappropriate. Existing links show the origin of individual recommendations, but do not confirm that the entire catalog has undergone clinical approval or that the algorithm has passed clinical validation.
